## 1. What inventory does Aston Martin hold, and how has it moved 2019 to 2026?

### Takeaway
Aston Martin's inventory note does not use the textbook "raw materials / WIP / finished goods" headings: it reports (i) parts for resale, service parts and production stock (which contains raw materials and bought-in components), (ii) work in progress and (iii) finished vehicles (including company-owned service cars). Group inventories rose from £165.3m (1 Jan 2019) to a peak of £353.7m at 30 June 2025, closing FY2025 at £277.7m and H1 2026 at £312.9m. Inventory days lengthened sharply in 2024 and 2025 as volumes fell.

### Inferences
- **Mapping to textbook categories:** "Production stock" ≈ raw materials and bought-in components; "work in progress" = cars on the line/in rectification at Gaydon and St Athan; "finished vehicles" = finished goods (new cars not yet wholesaled to dealers plus company service/demonstrator cars); "parts for resale/service parts" = aftersales (MRO-type) inventory. The single biggest category (c.58% of the total in 2023: 157.7/272.7) is parts and production stock, not finished cars, which is typical for an OEM that wholesales cars to dealers quickly but must hold long-tail spare parts for decades of models (inference from note data above).
- **WIP fell from £48.5m (2022) to £33.2m (2023)**, consistent with the unwinding of the 2022 "cars built awaiting parts" problem (DBX707s) described in the H1 2022 transcript.
- **Inventory days (calculated by this researcher; denominators differ, so compare within series only):**
  - Year-end inventory / implied cost of sales x 365: 2021 = **95.6 days**; 2022 = **112.2 days**; 2023 = **100.2 days**.
  - Year-end inventory / cost of inventories recognised as expense x 365: 2024 = **133.9 days**; 2025 = **155.5 days** (average-inventory basis: 2024 = 127.2 days; 2025 = 162.6 days). Inventory turnover on this basis fell from 2.73x (2024) to 2.35x (2025).
  - "Cost of inventories expensed" is narrower than cost of sales (it excludes e.g. some labour/overhead/warranty), so the 2024-25 figures overstate days relative to 2021-23; nevertheless the direction (slower turnover as wholesale volume fell 10% in 2025 while inventory stayed near £280-350m) is clear.
- The doubling of NRV write-downs (£4.2m to £9.8m) alongside targeted dealer support for "older inventory" in 2026 suggests some finished-vehicle/parts stock became harder to sell at full price during the 2025 demand slowdown (tariffs, China).
- The intra-year peak (£353.7m at 30 June 2025) versus year-end (£277.7m) shows strong seasonality: Aston Martin builds inventory in H1/Q3 and wholesales heavily in Q4, so year-end balance sheets flatter the average inventory investment.

## 2. Dealer/channel stock: destocking, demand-led "build-to-order", Valhalla and Specials

### Takeaway
Aston Martin has twice had to correct excess dealer stock: in 2019-2020 (190-unit destock in 2019, then a 1,580-unit cut in GT/Sport dealer inventory in 2020 as part of a shift to a "demand-led build-to-order model") and again in 2025-2026 after tariffs and China weakness (retail outpacing wholesale by more than 30% in H1 2026, with destocking "largely complete" by Q3 2026). Specials such as Valhalla (999 units) are fully pre-sold and deposit-funded, which is the purest form of build-to-order.

### Inferences
- Aston Martin wholesales to (largely independent) dealers, so reported revenue recognises a sale when the car leaves for the dealer. Dealer stock is therefore off Aston Martin's balance sheet but is a "hidden" channel inventory: when it is too high, Aston Martin must either cut wholesale volumes (lower revenue/absorption) or fund dealer support (discounts), both of which hit margins. This happened in 2019-20 and again in 2025-26.
- The pattern is consistent with the "bullwhip"/push-system critique in the build-to-order literature (Holweg & Pil, 2004): forecast-driven production to fill dealer stock leads to discounting, whereas order-driven production protects price.
- Specials (Valkyrie, Valour, Valiant, Valhalla) act as a negative working-capital model: deposits are collected before build, so customers finance inventory; unwinding of these deposits on delivery (e.g. -£178m in FY2024) is a major working-capital swing factor, often larger than the inventory movement itself.

## 3. Inventory control techniques in practice (JIT/sequencing, kanban, MRP/ERP, ABC, EOQ, safety stock, parts operations)

### Takeaway
Direct, citable evidence exists for: (a) **outsourced inbound logistics with sequencing and kitting to the assembly line** (DHL Supply Chain at Gaydon and St Athan, i.e. a just-in-sequence form of JIT); (b) **statistical safety-stock optimisation for ~80,000 service-part SKUs** using ToolsGroup software (18% inventory-value reduction, 97.1% first-time availability); (c) an ERP history with **Microsoft Dynamics AX** (chosen over SAP) and a current **Manufacturing Execution System** programme; and (d) a heritage parts operation at **Aston Martin Works, Newport Pagnell**. No public source names kanban, ABC classification, EOQ or MRP explicitly at Aston Martin; these are described below as typical applications, clearly labelled as inference.

### Cited Findings

**Technique definitions (textbook sources)**
- JIT: producing/delivering items only when needed, in the quantity needed, to minimise inventory and expose waste; kanban is a pull-control signal (card/container) authorising replenishment; MRP explodes the master production schedule through the bill of materials to calculate dependent-demand requirements; ERP integrates MRP with finance, sales and HR; EOQ = sqrt(2DS/H) balances ordering and holding costs for independent-demand items; ABC (Pareto) classification focuses control effort on the small share of items accounting for most annual usage value; safety stock buffers demand and lead-time uncertainty to a target service level. — Slack, Brandon-Jones & Burgess (2022) *Operations Management*, 10th edn, Pearson; Heizer, Render & Munson (2020) *Operations Management: Sustainability and Supply Chain Management*, 13th edn, Pearson. (Standard textbook definitions; page numbers not verified in this session; student should add page references from the editions held.)
- Build-to-order (BTO) vs build-to-stock: forecast-driven systems rely on high levels of finished vehicles in the market and the discounts needed to sell them; BTO links production to customer orders to reduce finished-vehicle stock. — [Holweg & Pil (2004), *The Second Century*, MIT Press](https://direct.mit.edu/books/book/1965/chapter/53740/Islands-of-Excellence); [Holweg & Hines (2002), SAE 2002-01-0176](https://saemobilus.sae.org/content/2002-01-0176/); [Holweg & Miemczyk (2002), *IJPDLM*, Emerald](https://www.emeraldinsight.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/09600030210455438/full/html)

### Inferences (how techniques are likely applied; labelled as researcher inference, not company disclosure)
- **JIT / just-in-sequence:** DHL's "sequencing, kitting ... delivery of parts to the assembly line" at St Athan is the defining feature of just-in-sequence supply; it implies minimal line-side stock, with parts presented in build order (important where each car is individually specified via "Q by Aston Martin" personalisation). At low volumes (c.5,000-7,000 cars/yr across two plants), pure supplier-direct JIT is less economical than in mass production, so a 3PL-run consolidation centre feeding sequenced kits is the typical compromise.
- **Kanban:** likely used for high-runner, low-value fasteners/consumables at line-side (two-bin or e-kanban), the usual pattern in automotive assembly, but no Aston Martin-specific evidence was found.
- **MRP/ERP:** a car with a deep bill of materials and dependent demand (body, AMG-sourced powertrain, interiors) would necessarily be planned via MRP logic within the ERP; the 2022 and 2024 "cars awaiting parts" episodes are the classic failure mode where one missing dependent-demand item halts completion and inflates WIP.
- **ABC classification:** with ~80,000 service SKUs and long tails of slow-moving heritage parts, ABC/XYZ-type segmentation is the standard approach; ToolsGroup's ML-derived "8 categories of demand behaviour" is effectively an advanced, demand-pattern-based segmentation that serves the same purpose.
- **EOQ:** applicable mainly to independent-demand consumables and service parts with stable usage; for production parts, lot sizes are set by MRP/supplier contracts rather than classic EOQ.
- **Safety stock:** directly evidenced in service parts (daily service-level-driven optimisation). The 2024 supplier disruption suggests the company's production-side buffers were insufficient for multi-supplier shocks; the trade-off is that higher buffers would further worsen the already long inventory days (Section 1).

Inventory practice has been a recurring driver of Aston Martin's cash burn and margin volatility: dealer over-stocking forced costly destocks (2019-20, 2025-26) that cut wholesale volumes and required dealer support; supply-chain parts shortages left near-finished cars in WIP (2022, 2023, 2024), shifting deliveries into Q4 and triggering guidance cuts; and slower turnover in 2024-25 tied up c.£280-350m of cash while free cash outflows were c.£390-410m a year. ### Inferences
- **Cash:** at year-end 2025, inventory of £277.7m equalled roughly two-thirds of that year's £410m free cash outflow; inventory peaked at £353.7m mid-2025, so the intra-year cash tied up was higher still. Every 10 days of inventory reduction at the 2025 cost-of-inventories run-rate (£651.9m) would release roughly £18m of cash (651.9/365 x 10). (Researcher calculation.)
- **Operational:** the recurring "built but awaiting parts" problem (2022 DBX707, 2023 DB12, 2024 late components) shows the downside of lean, sequenced inbound supply in a low-volume OEM with limited supplier leverage: shortages move output into Q4, inflate WIP and receivables at half-year, and cause guidance misses that damage investor confidence (e.g. -20% share move, Sept 2024).
- **Margin:** excess channel stock is resolved through dealer support/discounts, eroding realised prices (explicitly cited in the Q3 2026 statement), while destocking cuts wholesale volumes and therefore fixed-cost absorption (overheads are absorbed into inventory on a "normalised level of activity", so under-utilisation is expensed). Conversely, BTO Specials (Valhalla) lift gross margin (37.4% Q3 2026) and are deposit-financed.
- **Overall evaluation:** the 2020 shift to demand-led BTO improved pricing power and gross margin through 2023, but the company did not sustain channel discipline when demand softened in 2024-25 (tariffs, China), requiring a second destock. The service-parts operation (ToolsGroup) is the clearest example of sophisticated, effective inventory control (18% less inventory at higher availability); the vehicle supply chain is the weakest point.
