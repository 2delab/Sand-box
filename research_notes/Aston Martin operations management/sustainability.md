## 1. The "Racing. Green." strategy: targets, operations, materials and emissions data

### Takeaway
"Racing. Green." was announced on 22 April 2022, alongside the 2021 Sustainability Report. The brief's "launched 2021" date is slightly out: 2021 was the first reporting year. The strategy's headline targets were net-zero manufacturing by 2030, a 30% cut in supply-chain emissions by 2030 (from a 2020 base) and net zero across the whole value chain by 2039. By 2024–25 these had been quietly re-based:
- the reduction target became 42% by 2030 and 90% by 2050, from a 2022 base;
- AML withdrew from SBTi validation in 2025;
- the net-zero year is now stated as 2050.

Operational (Scope 1 and 2) emissions are falling, mainly because of renewable-electricity certificates and carbon credits. Scope 3, which is about 99% of the footprint, rose 32% between 2022 and 2025.

### Inferences
- **Targets slipped.** The net-zero date moved from 2039 (value chain) to 2050. Together with the 2030 "net-zero manufacturing" goal giving way to a 42% all-scope cut, and the SBTi withdrawal, this marks a substantive weakening of ambition between 2022 and 2025. AML does not present it that way. A strong point for critical evaluation.
- **Small and inflated base.** Scope 1+2 (about 8,000 t) is under 1% of the footprint, given Scope 3 above 1.1 Mt in 2023. Operational wins are real but marginal. The material problem, purchased goods and use-phase emissions of ICE cars, is moving the wrong way. Part of the 98% Scope 2 cut rests on REGOs and offsets, not physical decarbonisation.
- **"Carbon neutral manufacturing" relies on offsets.** The 2023 claim depends on certificates and credits. That makes it exposed under stricter UK and EU green-claims rules (CMA Green Claims Code; EU Green Claims and ECGT directives), though no regulatory action against AML was found.

### Gaps
- Waste-to-landfill, zero-waste-to-landfill status, water withdrawal and recycled-content percentages: in the primary PDFs, but not retrievable in this session.
- Whether the St Athan solar array was actually installed, and its MW capacity, by 2025–26.
- The Bridge of Weir low-carbon leather partnership: no source found linking it to AML. Do not assert it without verifying.
- Full Scope 3 totals for 2022, 2024 and 2025 in absolute tCO2e.
- Whether the SBTi dashboard ever listed AML with validated targets. The dashboard was not checked directly.

## 2. Electrification roadmap and its changes: implications for credibility

### Takeaway
AML's first BEV has slipped five times:
1. 2025 (the 2022 plan)
2. 2026 (announced 2024)
3. 2027
4. "by 2030"
5. 2033–2035 (CEO Adrian Hallmark, October 2026)

V8 and V12 engines are retained to 2035. EV capex was cut to fund survival. The Valhalla PHEV, delayed into late 2025, is now the electrification flagship. This is the single biggest credibility problem for "Racing. Green."

### Cited Findings
- **April 2022 plan:**
  - an electrified option (PHEV or BEV) on every product line by 2026;
  - the "entire core portfolio" of GT, sports cars and SUVs fully electrified by 2030;
  - first BEV in 2025.
  - — [Carscoops (2022)](https://www.carscoops.com/2022/04/aston-martin-pledges-for-a-fully-electrified-model-range-by-2030/); [electrive (2022)](https://www.electrive.com/2022/04/25/aston-martin-to-release-first-bev-model-in-2025/)
- **2024:** the first BEV was pushed from 2025 to 2026 in favour of PHEVs. Chairman Lawrence Stroll said demand "was not what we thought it was two years ago". — [Autocar (2024)](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/new-cars/aston-martin-delays-first-electric-car-2026-favour-phevs); [LBC (2024)](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/c56ccdef7e1e48eaa1adc5b65034bb3b-5HjcfLP_2/)
- **Intermediate dates:** 2027 and then "by 2030" were indicated under various leaders. — [Worthbury (2026)](https://worthbury.com/magazine/briefing/aston-martin-first-ev-delay-2033/)
- **October 2026:**
  - Hallmark: the first EV will come in 2033 or later, "could be 2035, it might be 2033… It's not 2031".
  - He described AML as "not BEV deniers but BEV delayers" and said the business will stay "predominantly ICE-based" until 2035.
  - V8 and V12 engines are kept to 2035.
  - The timing is tied to legislation in certain markets, not demand.
  - — [Autocar (2026)](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/new-cars/aston-wont-launch-ev-until-least-2033-will-keep-v8-and-v12); [Motor1 (2026)](https://www.motor1.com/news/810568/aston-martin-delays-first-ev/); [Robb Report (2026)](https://robbreport.com/motors/cars/aston-martin-first-ev-delay-until-2033-1238723511/); [Yahoo Finance (2026)](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/articles/aston-martin-says-first-ev-131800519.html)
- **Lucid (June 2023):**
  - Lucid agreed to supply EV powertrain components and battery technology.
  - AML would make phased cash payments and give Lucid a 3.7% stake.
  - — [TechCrunch (2023)](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/26/aston-martin-taps-lucid-to-help-develop-electric-vehicles)
- **Capex cut:** the five-year capex plan fell from £2bn to £1.7bn by delaying EV technology investment (reported 2025). — [WardsAuto (2026)](https://www.wardsauto.com/news/aston-martin-one-in-five-job-cuts/813044/); [Investing.com/Reuters (2025)](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/aston-martin-to-cut-5-of-global-workforce-3891547)
- **Valhalla PHEV:**
  - AML's first PHEV, limited to 999 units.
  - Deliveries slipped into H2 2025, with only about 150 expected by year-end 2025.
  - Q4 2025 wholesales rose 47% quarter-on-quarter to 2,096, "helped by early Valhalla deliveries".
  - A Valhalla Spider is due from Q3 2027.
  - — [HotCars (2025)](https://www.hotcars.com/valhalla-delay-derailed-aston-martins-comeback/); [Ticker/LSE RNS FY2025 (25 Feb 2026)](https://www.ticker.app/lse/AML/rns/2026-02-25/Preliminary-Results-FY2025/2503U); [Motor1 (2026)](https://www.motor1.com/news/813475/aston-martin-valhalla-spider-debuts/)

### Inferences
- The 2022 promise of a fully electrified core range by 2030 is now effectively abandoned for BEVs. It survives only if "electrified" is read as including PHEVs.
- Use-phase emissions (Scope 3 Category 11) cannot fall materially without BEVs. The 42%-by-2030 all-scope target therefore looks unattainable on the current product plan.
- The "BEV delayer" framing is candid. That may protect trust with enthusiast customers and investors focused on near-term margins, while eroding it with ESG investors and NGOs.

### Gaps
- Current status of the Lucid supply agreement (2025–26): no update found.
- Official AML statement (RNS or capital markets day) formally replacing the 2030 electrification target: not found. The 2033 date comes from CEO interviews.

## 3. Social responsibility: people, communities, supply chain, ratings and reporting

AML missed its 25%-by-2025 women-in-leadership target, at about 17% in 2025, broadly flat since 2022, and has reset the target to 30% by 2030. It has a Human Rights Policy and an annual Modern Slavery Statement. ESG ratings are mixed: CDP Climate improved to B, but MSCI was downgraded from A to BBB. Workforce cuts of about 20% in 2026 undercut the "people" pillar.

## 4. Aston Martin Aramco F1 team: sustainability links and separation from AML

### Takeaway
The F1 team runs its own "Make A Mark" ESG programme, with SBTi near-term (2030) and net-zero (2050) targets. It has a solar-roofed AMR Technology Campus and FIA three-star environmental accreditation. Its reporting is separate from AML's. It offers AML brand "halo", not AML emissions reductions.

### Cited Findings
- The team's "Make A Mark" ESG framework has three pillars, Environment, Belong and Community, with SBTi near-term targets for 2030 and net zero by 2050 (ESG Report 2025). — [Aston Martin Aramco F1 Team ESG Report 2025](https://downloads.astonmartinf1.com/MakeAMark_ESG_Report_2025.pdf)
- AMR Technology Campus:
  - the solar roof had generated 1.3 GWh since installation (ESG Report 2024);
  - FIA 3-Star Environmental Accreditation and ISO 20121 (sustainable events) achieved.
  - — [Aston Martin F1 ESG Report 2024](https://downloads.astonmartinf1.com/MakeAMark_ESG_Report_2024.pdf); [BSI (n.d.)](https://www.bsigroup.com/en-GB/insights-and-media/insights/blogs/aston-martin-aramco-f1-team-celebrates-bsi-certifications/)
- A guest document claims the campus design saves about 278 tCO2e a year. This is marketing material, not audited. — [AMRTC Guest Information (n.d.)](https://aston-martin-guest-services.slides.com/michaelasavage/amrtc-guest-information-11825f)
- Aramco partnership: the ambition is cars powered entirely by sustainable fuels, in line with F1's 2026 rules and the sport's net-zero-by-2030 goal. That goal is sport-wide, not team-specific. — [Sustainability Magazine (n.d.)](https://sustainabilitymag.com/renewable-energy/how-aramco-fuels-f1s-race-to-net-zero-with-aston-martin)

### Inferences
- The F1 team holds validated-style SBTi targets and visible infrastructure. AML withdrew from SBTi. This contrast can confuse stakeholders, who may credit the shared brand with the team's credentials.
- Sponsorship by Aramco, an oil major, is a recurring greenwashing critique of F1 generally. Treat this as an argument, not a sourced finding about AML.

### Gaps
- Ownership separation was not verified in this session. The F1 team, AMR GP Ltd, is understood to be controlled by Lawrence Stroll's consortium as a separate legal entity licensing the Aston Martin name, while Stroll's Yew Tree consortium is also AML's largest shareholder. Confirm via Companies House or AML AR 2025 (related-party notes) before stating it.

## 5. Effectiveness: progress against targets, criticism, stakeholder trust and long-term viability

### Takeaway
Measured against its own 2022 promises, "Racing. Green." has under-delivered.

| Area | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Net-zero manufacturing by 2030 | Replaced by a 42% cut |
| Net zero by 2039 | Now 2050 |

Financial distress is the binding constraint:
- 2025 pre-tax loss of £363.9m;
- net debt of £1.38bn at end-2025, rising to about £1.54bn by mid-2026;

The UK ZEV mandate's small-volume exemptions remove regulatory pressure to electrify. Long-term viability depends more on financial repair and on EU and US regulation in the 2030s than on the sustainability strategy.

### Inferences (for critical evaluation)
- **Triple bottom line (Elkington):** the "profit" leg is failing, and this is starving "planet" (EV capex cut by £300m) and "people" (20% redundancies). This illustrates that TBL commitments are fragile when financial sustainability is absent.
- **Stakeholder theory (Freeman):**
  - Investors and creditors, the salient, powerful stakeholders, have been prioritised over employees and environmental stakeholders.
  - Customer demand ("BEV delayers") is used to legitimise the delay.
  - Regulators have enabled it through the ZEV derogation.
  - Mitchell, Agle and Wood's salience framework (power, legitimacy, urgency) could explain why.
- **Trust by stakeholder group:**

| Group | Effect | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| ESG investors | Likely eroded | MSCI downgrade; SBTi exit |
| Disclosure-focused raters | Improved | CDP upgrade |
| Customers | Probably unaffected or positive | Ultra-luxury buyers favour ICE emotion |
| Employees | Damaged | Repeated cuts |
| Regulators | Accommodating, for now | ZEV exemption |

- **Greenwashing risk:** "carbon neutral manufacturing" rests on REGOs and offsets; the "Racing. Green." branding continues while targets were weakened. Exposure under the UK Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (CMA direct enforcement of consumer law from April 2025) is an inference, not a finding.
- **Long-term viability:** Selling ICE cars past 2035 in the UK and EU depends on derogations and on the e-fuel debate. A BEV launched in 2033–35 leaves little time to develop it, and Lucid-sourced technology may age. Viability hinges on recapitalisation, not ESG.
