## 1. Sequential stages of the build (Gaydon sports cars)

### Takeaway
Gaydon does not use a fast moving line. Cars are hand-built on an **indexed line of fixed workstations**: a bonded/riveted aluminium (VH-era) structure goes through the body shop, then a long paint and hand-polish process (up to ~50 hours, the largest block of the roughly 200 labour-hours per car), then trim and final assembly at about 39-minute stations. End-of-line checks are a settle/speed-bump run, a water-leak test, a rolling-road test (there is no test track on site, per trade press) and a final inspector sign-off recorded on a "Final Inspection By" plaque.

### Cited Findings

**Site context**
- Gaydon opened in 2003 on the former RAF Gaydon airfield. It was Aston Martin's first purpose-built facility, and all the company's sports cars are made there — [AstonMartins.com, "Aston Martin Gaydon (2003–date)"](https://astonmartins.com/factory-guide/aston-martin-gaydon-2003-date/)
- The site is described as 55 acres (only one source found) — [AstonMartins.com factory guide](https://astonmartins.com/factory-guide/aston-martin-gaydon-2003-date/)
- Ford (then the owner) planned the Gaydon plant in 2000 to consolidate production and R&D on the former Rover site acquired in the Land Rover deal. Ford also planned to raise output from ~1,000 to 2,500 units a year — [AM-Online, 2000](https://www.am-online.com/news/2000/8/18/third-aston-martin-planned/583/); [Fleet News, 2000](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/news/2000/8/22/cheaper-aston-martin-on-way/7928/)
- DB9 coupé series production began in January 2004 and the V8 Vantage in mid-2005 — [AstonMartins.com factory guide](https://astonmartins.com/factory-guide/aston-martin-gaydon-2003-date/)
- Second-generation Vantage production began at Gaydon in May 2018 — [AM-Online, 18 May 2018](https://www.am-online.com/news/car-manufacturer-news/2018/05/18/production-of-new-aston-martin-vantage-starts)
- In Nov 2018, 650 staff worked in Gaydon manufacturing: **250 in trim and final assembly, 260 in body shop and paint shop, 140 in off-track areas**. Models then built there were the Vantage, Rapide S, DB11 and DBS Superleggera — [Automotive Manufacturing Solutions (AMS), "Lap of luxury", Nov 2018](https://www.automotivemanufacturingsolutions.com/press-and-body/lap-of-luxury/534709)

**Line layout / flow principle**
- "Instead of a conventional moving assembly line, Gaydon makes its cars at successive workstations… technicians hand-build the cars. The line is an indexed one, and once work is completed at one station, the bodies are moved to the next station" — [Aston Martin, "Gaydon Headquarters", 22 Feb 2017](https://www.astonmartin.com/en-gb/our-world/news/2017/2/22/gaydon-headquarters) (also on [media.astonmartin.com](https://media.astonmartin.com/?p=271))
- Building an Aston takes place "at a less frenetic pace" than at factories where cars sit on a moving line and work is completed against the clock — [Automoblog, "A Private Tour of the Aston Martin Factory"](https://www.automoblog.com/aston-martin-factory-tour/) (undated tour account)
- Model-dependent material handling, reported in an older tour account: Vantage and Rapide rolled down a **motorised line**, while DB9 and Vanquish sat on **cradles pushed by hand** from station to station — returned with [Speedhunters 2013](https://www.speedhunters.com/2013/08/aston-martin-at-gaydon-the-dream-factory/) / [The Register 2015](https://www.theregister.com/2015/05/10/how_is_an_aston_martin_built/) search results (attribution uncertain; c.2013–2015)
- Operators "typically train up on three stations and rotate every so often" (multi-skilling and job rotation) — [Speedhunters, Aug 2013](https://www.speedhunters.com/2013/08/aston-martin-at-gaydon-the-dream-factory/)

**Stage 1 – Body structure / body-in-white (body shop)**
- VH-platform cars used **bonded and riveted aluminium monocoques**. In 2013 the monocoques were **built by a subcontractor and shipped to Gaydon** for assembly, and chassis were stored on racks until slotted into the line — [Speedhunters, Aug 2013](https://www.speedhunters.com/2013/08/aston-martin-at-gaydon-the-dream-factory/)
- Bonding adhesive was applied by "one of the few robots on the line". Panels were placed by hand in a jig and **cured with heat** — [Speedhunters, Aug 2013](https://www.speedhunters.com/2013/08/aston-martin-at-gaydon-the-dream-factory/)
- DB9 era: the body-in-white was a bonded aluminium monocoque, and the front wings, doors, bonnet and boot lid were **fitted by hand** — [AstonMartins.com, "DB9 Production at Gaydon" (c.2004)](https://astonmartins.com/db9-production-at-am-gaydon-20034/)
- An older Autocar report said Gaydon's **alloy-tub assembly section was working 24 hours a day on three shifts**, with no extra capacity possible without further investment — [Autocar, "Aston plans growth to match Bentley"](https://images.cdn.autocar.co.uk/car-news/industry/aston-plans-growth-match-bentley) (date not confirmed; pre-2016 context)
- Racing variants branch off at the body-in-white stage: chassis go to Aston Martin Racing for race-car build — [FIA WEC, "The best of British: Aston Martin from road to track", 2022](https://www.fiawec.com/en/news/the-best-of-british-aston-martin-from-road-to-track/2022)

**Stage 2 – Paint shop**
- "The deep lustre of every Aston Martin takes **up to 50 hours** to achieve". All cars are **polished back once lacquered and re-buffed** to create mirror-like finishes — [Aston Martin, "Gaydon Headquarters", 2017](https://www.astonmartin.com/en-gb/our-world/news/2017/2/22/gaydon-headquarters)
- A Vanquish required **50–60 hours in the paint shop** alone (2013) — [Speedhunters, Aug 2013](https://www.speedhunters.com/2013/08/aston-martin-at-gaydon-the-dream-factory/)
- About a quarter of the ~200 build hours goes to painting — [Supercar Blondie, "Inside the exclusive Aston Martin supercar factory"](https://supercarblondie.com/aston-martin-supercar-factory-handcrafted-luxury/) (undated); SlashGear also cites ~50 paint hours — [SlashGear, "Who Owns Aston Martin And Where Are The Cars Built?"](https://www.slashgear.com/1759441/aston-martin-who-owns-where-built/)
- DB9 era (c.2004): cars were spray painted by hand, taking **up to 25 hours per car**, with bumpers and sills painted alongside the shell. Process: **two colour-matched primer coats, each hand-flatted → colour coat → clear coat → clear coat hand-polished → re-inspection**, in an enclosed dust-free area — [AstonMartins.com, "DB9 Production at Gaydon"](https://astonmartins.com/db9-production-at-am-gaydon-20034/)
- Owner forum account: special/custom colours are **hand sprayed**, while standard colours are **robot sprayed** — [6SpeedOnline forum, "Aston Martin Factory Tour"](https://www.6speedonline.com/forums/aston-martin/357785-aston-martin-factory-tour.html) (anecdotal, undated)
- Conflicting/unattributed range: one search extract quoted **"between 50 and 70 hours"** to paint and finish a body depending on colour and finish (attribution uncertain).

**Stage 3 – Trim (leather/interior)**
- Leather sports seats are **hand-stitched** in the factory — [carsales.com.au, "Best of British: Aston Martin – the factory Bond built"](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/best-of-british-aston-martin-the-factory-bond-built-104930/)
- Craft method (described at Aston Martin Works, Newport Pagnell, the heritage/restoration arm, **not** Gaydon series production): modern cars use **Bridge of Weir** leather. Pieces are vetted, **hand-cut to long-established patterns**, sewn with heavy-duty thread onto a muslin underlay on a foot-pedal machine, with some hand finishing — [Hagerty UK, "Lending a hand at Aston Martin Works"](https://www.hagerty.co.uk/articles/hard-craft-articles/lending-a-hand-at-aston-martin-works/)
- One extract reported "70 man-hours are taken on trim" (attribution uncertain; likely the carsales.com.au feature above). Treat as unverified.

**Stage 4 – Final assembly (incl. powertrain marriage)**
- In 2018-era coverage, the **painted body shell is placed at the beginning of the production line** and passes through stations of **about 40 minutes each** (attribution uncertain; consistent with Autocar's 39-minute figure below)
- Gaydon assembly takt was **39 minutes**, against a planned **20 minutes** at St Athan — [Autocar, "Aston Martin's St Athan plant to boost manufacturing efficiency", Nov 2018](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/new-cars/aston-martins-st-athan-plant-boost-manufacturing-efficiency) (print version: [Autocar, "Efficiency key at St Athan plant", 14 Nov 2018, via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/autocar/20181114/283966063509087))
  - **Conflict:** AMS says the 39 minutes is the **body-shop** takt (slow gluing and curing of aluminium), while paint and final assembly run at 20 minutes. This was said about St Athan's design — [AMS, "Made in Wales"](https://www.automotivemanufacturingsolutions.com/strategy/made-in-wales/532503)
  - **Conflict:** a 2015 Register visit described a Gaydon **"heartbeat" of 25 minutes** — [The Register, "How is an Aston Martin built?", 10 May 2015](https://www.theregister.com/2015/05/10/how_is_an_aston_martin_built/)
- Engines are supplied as complete units (see Q4 below). The Vantage uses a 4.0-litre twin-turbo V8 "supplied by Mercedes-AMG, and built by the automaker in Germany" — [Motor Authority, "Here's how the Aston Martin Vantage is made"](https://www.motorauthority.com/news/1129368_here-s-how-the-aston-martin-vantage-is-made)

**Stage 5 – End-of-line test and inspection**
- Owner tour account: finished cars are **driven over a series of speed bumps "to settle everything in"**, then driven to a garage where **2,400 gallons of water** are dumped on them to check for leaks. Each car then goes to another building where "a track experience can be simulated. They all used to go to the track, but now it is all simulated with electronics" — [6SpeedOnline forum, "Aston Martin Factory Tour"](https://www.6speedonline.com/forums/aston-martin/357785-aston-martin-factory-tour.html) (undated; mentions DB9 and One-77, so probably c.2010–2013; anecdotal)
- Trade-press account: "Before its final sign off by an inspector and the almost ceremonial placing of the Aston Martin moniker on the front, **every car is subjected to a rolling road test** which replicates anything that can be done on the road". The author notes **there is no test track at Gaydon** — [Trade Unique Cars, "Aston Martin Factory Tour"](https://tradeuniquecars.com.au/features/1710/aston-martin-factory-tour) (date not confirmed)
- **Final inspection plaque:** under the bonnet, reading "HAND BUILT IN ENGLAND – FINAL INSPECTION BY [name]" — [Aston-Martin.com, "Meeting the maker: Barry Griffin", 25 Jul 2012](https://www.aston-martin.com/2012/07/25/meeting-the-maker-barry-griffin/); [Aston-Martin.com tag "Final Inspection By"](https://www.aston-martin.com/tag/final-inspection-by/); the plaque is also sold as a part — [Aston Martin Store, "Final Inspection Plate"](https://www.astonstore.co.uk/product/final-inspection-plt/)
- Owner forum reports: the plaque normally carries the inspector's name, some special editions (e.g. GT, N430) lack a name, and CEO Andy Palmer signed off early cars — [PistonHeads, "So who did your 'Final Inspection'?"](https://www.pistonheads.com/gassing/topic.asp?t=1043857&i=120) (anecdotal)

### Inferences
- **Synthesised process flow for the flowchart** (inferred ordering. Each step is supported above, but the exact current sequence is not published as one list):
  1. Receive aluminium structure / extrusions / castings (historically, pre-built monocoques from a subcontractor)
  2. Body shop: adhesive application (robot) → hand placement in jig → riveting → **oven cure** → fit closures (wings, doors, bonnet, bootlid) by hand → BIW inspection
  3. [Branch: racing chassis to AMR]
  4. Paint shop: clean/prep → primer ×2 with hand flatting → base/colour coat (robot for standard colours, hand spray for special/Q colours) → clear coat → cure → hand flat and **polish/re-buff** → paint inspection
  5. Trim and final assembly (indexed stations, ~39-min takt c.2018): painted body loaded → wiring/interior/trim (parallel trim-shop feeder: hide cut → stitch → seats/panels) → **engine/powertrain marriage** (bought-in AMG V8 or Aston V12 from Cologne) → wheels, fluids, closures
  6. Settle run (speed bumps) → **water/monsoon leak test** → **rolling-road test** (no track on site) → rectification if needed
  7. Final inspection and sign-off → bonnet badge fitted → "Final Inspection By" plaque → (PDI/dispatch, see gaps)
- The paint block (25–60 hours depending on era/model) is clearly the largest single time block, and probably the main **lead-time driver** in a value-stream map. The body-shop cure step is the main **takt constraint** (per St Athan evidence).
- Inference: 650 manufacturing staff (2018) × 200 labour-hours per car is broadly consistent with ~15–20 cars/day on one-to-two shifts. This is a sanity check, not a sourced figure.

### Gaps
- No published stage list, station count or takt for the **current DB12 / 2024 Vantage / 2024 Vanquish** lines (post-2023 "new architecture" era).
- No source on **Valhalla** (mid-engine, carbon tub) build process at Gaydon.
- Whether Gaydon now builds tubs in-house or still receives them from a supplier was not confirmed for current models.
- Number of stations on the Gaydon trim and final line: not found.
- PDI and dispatch/logistics steps after final inspection: not found.
- Whether the water test, rolling road and "200 km test drive" are current practice: not confirmed. No source for a "200 km" road test was found.

## 2. Hours per car, stations, takt, cars per day, shifts, capacity

### Takeaway
The most-cited figure is **~200 labour-hours per Gaydon sports car** (company page, 2017), of which ~50 are paint. Gaydon ran at roughly **15–20 cars/day**, with a takt of ~39 minutes in 2018 (25-minute "heartbeat" in 2015). The St Athan DBX takes **~85 hours end-to-end** with a 20-minute takt (40 minutes in body/tub). Capacity figures conflict (Gaydon 5,000–7,500/year; St Athan 4,000–7,000/year).

### Cited Findings
- "Craftsmen at the Gaydon factory take **around 200 man-hours** to produce each Aston Martin sports car" — [Aston Martin, "Gaydon Headquarters", 22 Feb 2017](https://www.astonmartin.com/en-gb/our-world/news/2017/2/22/gaydon-headquarters); repeated by [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1759441/aston-martin-who-owns-where-built/)
- Cars per day: "Aston Martin build and sell almost 4,000 cars a year, which means **about 15–20 cars** roll out of Gaydon every day" — [Speedhunters, 2013](https://www.speedhunters.com/2013/08/aston-martin-at-gaydon-the-dream-factory/). Another 2014-era source gave 15/day (attribution uncertain).
- A 2024 visit report says the factory "on average produces **20 cars a day**" — [Fellowship of the Motor Industry, "Tour of Aston Martin factory" (2024)](https://motorfellowship.com/event/tour-of-aston-martin-factory/)
- Takt: Gaydon 39 min vs St Athan 20 min (2018) — [Autocar, Nov 2018](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/new-cars/aston-martins-st-athan-plant-boost-manufacturing-efficiency). Gaydon 25-min heartbeat (2015) — [The Register, 2015](https://www.theregister.com/2015/05/10/how_is_an_aston_martin_built/)
- Autocar (2018): St Athan "will make **7000 cars per year, the same number as Gaydon but on one instead of two lines**". St Athan workers would have "half the time to fit components compared with those at Gaydon" — [Autocar, Nov 2018](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/new-cars/aston-martins-st-athan-plant-boost-manufacturing-efficiency). This is the only source for "two lines" at Gaydon.
- Gaydon capacity conflicts: "capacity to produce 5,000 cars a year" (2004 report); "ample space to manufacture up to 7,500 cars a year"; a later Autocar piece said the plant was "stretched close to its maximum 7000 unit capacity" — [AstonMartins.com factory guide](https://astonmartins.com/factory-guide/aston-martin-gaydon-2003-date/); [Autocar, "Aston plans growth to match Bentley"](https://images.cdn.autocar.co.uk/car-news/industry/aston-plans-growth-match-bentley)
- Shifts: Gaydon tub assembly ran 24 hours/3 shifts (older Autocar report) — [Autocar](https://images.cdn.autocar.co.uk/car-news/industry/aston-plans-growth-match-bentley). An owner forum recalled a **shift change around 2pm** — [AstonMartinLife forum, "Factory tour Gaydon"](https://www.astonmartinlife.com/threads/factory-tour-gaydon.15099/) (anecdotal)
- St Athan: DBX takes "**85 hours, if it all comes through without stopping**. The slowest part is the curing, everything else is around 20 minutes takt time, but it's **40 minutes in body and tub, hence the two shift patterns**" (Scott Ward, Director of Manufacturing) — [AMS, "Scott Ward, director of manufacturing, Aston Martin St Athan"](https://www.automotivemanufacturingsolutions.com/oems/scott-ward-director-of-manufacturing-aston-martin-st-athan/544684) (c.2019–2020; interview by Nick Gibbs)
- St Athan annual output was expected to be ~4,000 units (paultan citing Autocar, 2020) — [paultan.org, "Aston Martin DBX production commences at St Athan", 10 Jul 2020](https://paultan.org/2020/07/10/aston-martin-dbx-production-commences-at-st-athan/). This conflicts with Autocar's 7,000 capacity figure. Inference: 4,000 was planned volume and 7,000 was capacity.
- Group output context: 2023 volume guidance cut to ~6,700 from ~7,000 — [CNBC, 1 Nov 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/01/aston-martin-shares-plunge-16percent-after-volume-target-cut.html). 2024 guidance cut to ~6,000 from 7,000. FY2024 wholesales 6,030 (−9%) — [Business Matters](https://bmmagazine.co.uk/news/aston-martin-shares-slump-after-profit-warning-amid-supply-chain-struggles/); [Hargreaves Lansdown](https://hl.co.uk/shares/share-research/aston-martin-lagonda-cutting-headcount-after-a-tough-2024)

### Inferences
- The "85 hours" (St Athan) and "200 hours" (Gaydon) are **different measures**. 85 h appears to be elapsed throughput time (flow time) for a DBX, while 200 h is labour content. They should not be compared directly in a VSM. Labour hours ≠ lead time.
- At a 39-minute takt over ~7.5 effective hours/shift, one line gives ~11–12 cars/shift. Two lines or two shifts would give ~20+/day, consistent with the "15–20/day" and "20/day" reports. This is an inference for illustration only.
- Halving takt at St Athan (20 min) implies roughly double the stations, or half the work content per station, versus Gaydon for a similar build. Work content per station is inferred, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No official current figures for Gaydon line count, number of stations, shift pattern, or daily rate for 2023–2026.
- No hours-per-car figure for DB12/Vanquish/Valhalla specifically.
- The 2015 (25 min) vs 2018 (39 min) takt discrepancy is unresolved. It may reflect different models/lines or different measurement points.

## 3. St Athan (DBX) — how it differs

### Takeaway
St Athan, in South Wales, is a converted MoD site of three "super hangars", one each for **body-in-white, paint and final assembly**. It opened officially in Dec 2019, the first production DBX came off the line in July 2020, and it is the sole DBX plant. It was designed with Gaydon's lessons: 20-minute takt, three separate curing ovens, and a three-storey, more automated paint shop with robots applying primer, base and clear, plus manual cleaning and polishing.

### Cited Findings
- 90-acre site with **three converted 'super-hangars'** from MoD St Athan. Fit-out started late 2018, ahead of pre-production in 2019 — [Torque (Singapore), "Aston Martin begins work to convert St Athan Super Hangars"](https://www.torque.com.sg/news/aston-martin-begins-work-convert-st-athan-super-hangars-manufacturing-facility/); [Aston Martin, "St Athan: AML's second production facility nears completion", 9 Nov 2018](https://www.astonmartin.com/en-us/our-world/news/2018/11/9/st-athan-aston-martin-lagonda-s-second-production-facility-nears-completion)
- Hangar functions: "the first a **body-in-white** plant, the second a **paint shop** and the third a **final assembly** hall" — [The Manufacturer](https://www.themanufacturer.com/articles/aston-martin-second-uk-facility-huge-vote-confidence-bringing-700-jobs/)
- Timeline: pre-production cars from **June 2019** ("ready to go into full production in H1 2020") — [Aston Martin media, "Aston Martin produces first cars at St Athan", 17 Jun 2019](https://media.astonmartin.com/aston-martin-produces-first-cars-at-st-athan/). **Official opening 6 Dec 2019** — [Aston Martin, "AML opens manufacturing plant in St Athan, Wales", 6 Dec 2019](https://www.astonmartin.com/en-us/our-world/news/2019/12/6/aston-martin-lagonda-opens-manufacturing-plant-in-st-athan-wales). **First production DBX July 2020** — [paultan.org, 10 Jul 2020](https://paultan.org/2020/07/10/aston-martin-dbx-production-commences-at-st-athan/); [PES Media, 9 Jul 2020](https://www.pesmedia.com/aston-martin-dbx-st-athan-09072020)
- Role: sole production facility for the DBX. Gaydon remains home of sports-car production — [Aston Martin media, Dec 2019](https://media.astonmartin.com/aston-martin-lagonda-opens-manufacturing-plant-in-st-athan-wales/). Around 80% of output expected to be exported — [Automotive World, Dec 2019](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news/aston-martin-lagonda-opens-manufacturing-plant-in-st-athan-wales/)
- Investment: St Athan chosen as part of a **£200m** investment in products and facilities. An analyst estimated fit-out of the body shop, paint shop and assembly halls at ~£60m (not company-confirmed) — [S&P Global AutoTechInsight, "Aston Martin decides location of new plant"](https://autotechinsight.spglobal.com/news/34733/-aston-martin-decides-location-of-new-plant). Contracts worth £60m to Welsh firms — [The Manufacturer](https://www.themanufacturer.com/articles/aston-martin-st-athan-contracts-worth-60m-to-welsh-firms/)
- Employment: 200 jobs created by opening, up to 550 more expected. ~600 workforce, ~750 at full production — [The Manufacturer](https://www.themanufacturer.com/articles/aston-martin-second-uk-facility-huge-vote-confidence-bringing-700-jobs/); [AMS, "Made in Wales"](https://www.automotivemanufacturingsolutions.com/strategy/made-in-wales/532503). Recruitment drew 3,000+ applicants, and the first 40 hires were **trained at Gaydon** — [Torque](https://www.torque.com.sg/news/aston-martin-begins-work-convert-st-athan-super-hangars-manufacturing-facility/)
- Body shop: three lines, **underframe (tub), body-in-white and finishing**. "It is a slow business gluing aluminium parts together, which is why the takt time [in] the bodyshop is 39 minutes compared to 20 minutes in the paintshop and final assembly" — [AMS, "Made in Wales"](https://www.automotivemanufacturingsolutions.com/strategy/made-in-wales/532503)
- Curing: "The bonded aluminium chassis for the DBX has to be oven cured. There are **three separate ovens** for curing. 'They're separate because of **lessons learned from Gaydon**,' says Scott Ward" — [AMS, "Made in Wales"](https://www.automotivemanufacturingsolutions.com/strategy/made-in-wales/532503)
- Paint shop: built upwards as a **three-storey** structure because of hangar height. The **ground floor is given over to manual cleaning** of bodies (two robots assist cleaning), and **robot sprayers are on the second floor**: **three Motoman robots** (installed by **Eisenmann**) apply **primer, base coat and clear coat**. Workers do a final polish for optional mirror finishes — [AMS, "Made in Wales"](https://www.automotivemanufacturingsolutions.com/strategy/made-in-wales/532503)
- Scott Ward has been in charge since 2017, promoted from director of production for body construction and paint shops at Gaydon — [AMS, Scott Ward interview](https://www.automotivemanufacturingsolutions.com/oems/scott-ward-director-of-manufacturing-aston-martin-st-athan/544684)
- "St Athan will be more efficient because it's a new plant being set up with the benefit of 15 years of experience of operations at Gaydon" (Aston Martin) — [Autocar, Nov 2018](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/new-cars/aston-martins-st-athan-plant-boost-manufacturing-efficiency)
- Disruptions: temporary halt announced March 2020 (COVID-19) — [Glamorgan Gazette via PressReader, 26 Mar 2020](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/glamorgan-gazette/20200326/281655372165862). 95 job losses at St Athan (reported c. March 2021) — [Autocar](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/business-tech,-development-and-manufacturing/aston-martin-confirms-95-job-losses-st-athan); [Girlracer, 11 Mar 2021](https://girlracer.co.uk/2021/03/11/job-losses-at-aston-martin-at-st-athan/). The early-2026 cut of up to 20% of the workforce (~600 roles) affects factory staff including St Athan — [AOL/BBC report](https://www.aol.com/articles/aston-martin-cuts-20-workforce-100941736.html); [Warwickshire Business, Feb 2026](https://business.warwickshire.gov.uk/february-2026/featured-news)

### Inferences
- In a VSM, St Athan's flow is **BIW (40-min takt, two shift patterns) → paint (20-min) → final assembly (20-min)**. The body shop runs extra shift time to feed downstream stages at the faster rate, a classic capacity-balancing approach to a cure-time bottleneck.
- The vertical, more robotised paint shop is a key differentiator from Gaydon's historically hand-spray-heavy paint process. The separate ovens reduce the risk that a single cure oven halts the whole flow (a single point of failure).

### Gaps
- No published station count for the St Athan final assembly line, and no DBX end-of-line test details (water test, rolling road), though they are presumably similar to Gaydon.
- Actual achieved annual output at St Athan (vs. 4,000 planned / 7,000 capacity) was not confirmed from production data.
- Whether St Athan now builds anything besides DBX (earlier plans mentioned EVs/Lagonda) is not confirmed.

## 4. Powertrain supply (engines)

### Takeaway
V8 models (Vantage, DB11/DB12 V8, DBX/DBX707) use **Mercedes-AMG 4.0 L twin-turbo V8 (M177 family)** engines supplied from Germany. The **5.2 L twin-turbo V12** was assembled at Ford's Cologne engine plant in a **separate facility staffed by Aston Martin employees**. Engines arrive complete for marriage on the assembly line.

### Cited Findings
- Mercedes-AMG: Aston has used AMG technology since 2017. "the Vantage and DB11 coupés and roadsters as well as the DBX SUV are powered by eight-cylinder engines from Mercedes-AMG" — [Mercedes-AMG, "AMG engines at other manufacturers"](https://www.mercedes-amg.com/en/amg-engines-at-other-manufacturers)
- Vantage V8 "supplied by Mercedes-AMG, and built by the automaker in Germany" — [Motor Authority](https://www.motorauthority.com/news/1129368_here-s-how-the-aston-martin-vantage-is-made). The original 2013 deal was reported by [CAR Magazine, 2013](https://www.carmagazine.co.uk/car-news/industry-news/aston-martin/aston-martin-to-use-mercedes-amg-v8-engines-2013/)
- V12: "built at Ford's engine plant in Cologne, albeit in a separate facility staffed by Aston Martin employees". Production would "eventually move" to a new UK facility (plan at the time) — [Car Throttle, "Aston Martin will ditch AMG V8s for self-built V6s, V12 safe (for now)"](https://www.carthrottle.com/news/aston-martin-will-ditch-amg-v8s-self-built-v6s-v12-safe-now)

### Inferences
- For flowcharting, engines are a **bought-in sub-assembly feeder stream** (AMG Affalterbach area for V8; Cologne for V12) that joins at the "powertrain marriage" station of final assembly. The long-distance inbound supply suggests buffer stock and an inbound logistics lead time in the VSM.

### Gaps
- Whether the V12 (e.g. Vanquish 2024, DBX S) is still assembled in Cologne as of 2026 was **not confirmed**. Sources are 2013–2020.
- The Valhalla's hybrid V8 (AMG-derived, flat-plane crank) supply route was not found.
- No source found on whether engines are dressed or tested at Gaydon before marriage.

## 5. Quality control, inspection and lean practices

### Takeaway
Documented QC points are paint inspection after polishing, end-of-line settle run, water-leak test, rolling-road test, and **named final-inspector sign-off** (bonnet plaque). Lean is evidenced mainly through CEO Andy Palmer's stated **kaizen** philosophy (ex-Nissan), operator multi-skilling/rotation, an indexed station line and takt time. No sources were found for andon, 5S or poka-yoke at Aston specifically.

### Cited Findings
- DB9 era: clear coat hand-polished and **inspected again** afterwards — [AstonMartins.com, DB9 production](https://astonmartins.com/db9-production-at-am-gaydon-20034/)
- Final sign-off by an inspector and fitting of the badge after a rolling-road test — [Trade Unique Cars](https://tradeuniquecars.com.au/features/1710/aston-martin-factory-tour)
- "Final Inspection By" plaque naming the inspector — [Aston-Martin.com, 2012](https://www.aston-martin.com/2012/07/25/meeting-the-maker-barry-griffin/)
- CEO Andy Palmer personally inspected **the first 1,000 DB11s** (2016–17), applying an inspection plaque to each car he approved, inspecting in the evenings after office hours — [Autocar, "Inspecting the first Aston Martin DB11s with CEO Andy Palmer"](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/new-cars/inspecting-first-aston-martin-db11s-ceo-andy-palmer); [Wikipedia, "Aston Martin DB11"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_Martin_DB11)
- Palmer: "I'm a disciple of Kaizen" (learned at Nissan: "if it's broken, fix it and make sure it never happens again"). He said "There's a lot of kaizen here" about Aston — [Aston Martin Magazine, "Keeping in Touch: An Interview with CEO Andy Palmer"](https://magazine.astonmartin.com/people/keeping-touch-interview-ceo-andy-palmer); [Great British Life, "Big CEO interview: Dr Andy Palmer"](https://greatbritishlife.co.uk/people/local-businesses/22582621.big-ceo-interview-dr-andy-palmer-aston-martin)

## 6. Customisation (Q by Aston Martin) and order-to-delivery

### Takeaway
Customisation enters at **order specification**. **Q Collection** options are chosen at ordering and add no build lead time. **Q Commission** projects are agreed **before production** and carry longer lead times (a dealer estimates weeks to several months). Gaydon builds largely to customer order, and "rarely two cars leaving the factory are identical".

### Cited Findings
- "The Q Collection offers a range of exclusive off-the-shelf options that are available at the point of ordering a car and **don't require an additional build lead time**". "Commission projects are often complex to create and are accordingly **agreed prior to production**" — [Aston Martin Magazine, "By special request: Q by Aston Martin's tailor-made enhancements"](https://magazine.astonmartin.com/design/special-request-q-aston-martins-tailor-made-enhancements)
- Q Commission can begin at a local dealership, **Gaydon HQ** or the **Mayfair** brand centre — [Aston Martin Magazine](https://magazine.astonmartin.com/design/special-request-q-aston-martins-tailor-made-enhancements); launch of Q Commission — [Autocar, "Aston Martin to launch Q Commission service"](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/motor-shows-geneva-motor-show/aston-martin-launch-q-commission-service-bespoke-models); [AutoGuide, Feb 2017](https://www.autoguide.com/auto-news/2017/02/-q-by-aston-martin-takes-customization-to-the-next-level.html)
- Dealer estimate: simple personalisation "might add a few weeks" to standard production times, while extensive bespoke work "can require several months". The finished vehicle goes through quality inspections before shipping to the dealer for handover — [Galpin Aston Martin, "Q by Aston Martin Customization Guide"](https://www.galpinastonmartin.com/q-by-aston-martin-customization-guide-los-angeles/) (dealer source, not official)
- Customer process at dealers: meeting with sales associates to decide between a wholly new model and an upgrade of an existing one — [Aston Martin Palm Beach, "What is Aston Martin Q?"](https://www.astonmartinpalmbeach.com/aston-martin-information/what-is-aston-martin-q/)
- 2024 visit: customers can see "the range of 60 colour schemes, samples of leathers etc." and "there are rarely two cars leaving the factory that are identical" — [Fellowship of the Motor Industry, 2024](https://motorfellowship.com/event/tour-of-aston-martin-factory/)
- Customer factory tours are arranged through dealers (free for existing owners per AMOC forum) — [AMOC forum](https://forum.amoc.org/post/aston-martin-factory-tour-9691742); [Aston Martin offers Gaydon factory tour – Manufacturing Management](https://www.manufacturingmanagement.co.uk/content/news/aston-martin-offers-gaydon-factory-tour/)

### Inferences
- Order-to-delivery flow (inferred): customer configures (online configurator / dealer) → dealer places order → [optional Q Commission design consultation and sign-off] → production slot allocated → build at Gaydon/St Athan → final inspection → shipping/logistics → dealer PDI → handover. The "agreed prior to production" rule implies a **specification freeze** before the build slot.
- Special/Q paint colours being hand-sprayed (forum) implies bespoke orders take a different, longer path in the paint shop. That is a plausible variation point in a flowchart.

### Gaps
- No official standard order-to-delivery lead time or production-slot scheduling method (e.g. order bank, frozen window) was found.
- No published description of the dealer PDI or dispatch/shipping process.

## 7. Bottlenecks and problems reported

### Takeaway
Structurally, the **body-shop adhesive cure** is the takt bottleneck (39–40 min vs 20 min elsewhere), and paint is the largest time block. Operationally, the main recent disruptions came from **supplier readiness and the new electrical/electronic (EE) platform for the DB12 in 2023**, **supplier failures (seat suppliers Recaro, Eissmann) in 2024**, logistics delays in 2022, and demand/tariff-driven cuts in 2025–26.

### Cited Findings
- Cure bottleneck: "The slowest part is the curing… it's 40 minutes in body and tub, hence the two shift patterns" — [AMS, Scott Ward interview](https://www.automotivemanufacturingsolutions.com/oems/scott-ward-director-of-manufacturing-aston-martin-st-athan/544684)
- Gaydon tub assembly at capacity (24h, 3 shifts) in older reporting — [Autocar](https://images.cdn.autocar.co.uk/car-news/industry/aston-plans-growth-match-bentley)
- Q3 2023: "The DB12 production ramp up was temporarily affected as **supplier readiness and integration of the new EE platform** that supports the fully redeveloped infotainment system was delayed". 2023 volume guidance was cut to ~6,700 from ~7,000 and shares fell — [CNBC, 1 Nov 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/01/aston-martin-shares-plunge-16percent-after-volume-target-cut.html); [Automotive News, "Aston Martin reports quarterly loss as DB12 output hits snags"](https://www.autonews.com/automakers/aston-martin-reports-quarterly-loss-db12-output-hits-snags/). Lawrence Stroll later said the software problem was fixed — [Automotive News](https://www.autonews.com/automakers/aston-martin-boss-lawrence-stroll-says-software-issue-fixed/)
- 2024: supply-chain issues disrupted production of four recently upgraded models. Issues were "now resolved but did impact Q3 volume and our full-year production capacity". Guidance was cut from 7,000 to 6,000, with reports linking this to insolvencies at German seat suppliers **Recaro and Eissmann** — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/retail/aston-martin-cuts-annual-delivery-outlook-on-supply-chain-issues-1.99122184); [Business Matters](https://bmmagazine.co.uk/news/aston-martin-shares-slump-after-profit-warning-amid-supply-chain-struggles/); [Hargreaves Lansdown](https://www.hl.co.uk/shares/share-research/aston-martin-lagonda-downgrades-2024-profit-outlook)
- FY2024 wholesales 6,030 (−9%), with a 5% workforce cut — [Hargreaves Lansdown](https://hl.co.uk/shares/share-research/aston-martin-lagonda-cutting-headcount-after-a-tough-2024)
- 2022: supply-chain and logistics disruption delayed deliveries, contributing to a £106m working-capital outflow (attribution uncertain; reported in Hargreaves Lansdown coverage) — [Hargreaves Lansdown, "Aston Martin ongoing supply chain issues dent performance"](https://www.hl.co.uk/shares/share-research/Aston-Martin-ongoing-supply-chain-issues-dent-performan)
- 2025: ~170 job cuts (~150 UK) and EV delay — [ITV News, 26 Feb 2025](https://www.itv.com/news/central/2025-02-26/aston-martin-cuts-170-jobs-after-slump-in-sales). Early 2026: up to 20% of workforce (~600) to be cut after 2025 net loss of £493.2m, linked to US tariffs and weak China demand — [AOL (BBC)](https://www.aol.com/articles/aston-martin-cuts-20-workforce-100941736.html); [Warwickshire Business, Feb 2026](https://business.warwickshire.gov.uk/february-2026/featured-news)
- 2019: Brexit contingency planning (stockpiling of parts) — [Global Times via PressReader, 12 Jan 2019](https://www.pressreader.com/china/global-times-weekend/20190112/281608126583848)

### Inferences
- For a VSM, mark two bottleneck types: (1) **internal constraint**, the body/tub cure (fixed process time); and (2) **external constraint**, single-source supplier parts (seats, EE components/software) that can starve final assembly. Software/EE integration is a **new class of bottleneck** for the new-architecture cars (DB12 onwards).
- The user brief mentions "incomplete cars parked awaiting parts". This is consistent with the 2023–24 supplier problems, but **no source explicitly describing parked incomplete cars was found**.

### Gaps
- No direct reporting found on the physical holding of incomplete cars awaiting parts, or on quality holds at Gaydon.
- No source found specifically on paint-shop capacity constraints in the current era.
- Annual-report (FY2023/FY2024/FY2025) primary text could not be retrieved (annualreports.com and astonmartinlagonda.com were not reachable). Figures above come from secondary financial press.
