## Q1. Company profile: industry, sites, products, volumes, ownership — and what is built where?

### Takeaway
Aston Martin Lagonda (LSE: AML) is a UK ultra-luxury, low-volume sports-car/GT/SUV maker (c.5,400–6,600 wholesales a year, 2021–2025) with headquarters and front-engined sports/GT production at Gaydon (Warwickshire), DBX SUV production at St Athan (South Wales, opened 2020), and heritage/restoration/continuation work at Aston Martin Works, Newport Pagnell. It has been controlled since 2020 by Lawrence Stroll's Yew Tree consortium (c.33% by May 2025), which has repeatedly recapitalised it.

### Inferences
- Aston Martin is a classic low-volume / high-variety, high-value operation (roughly 25–30 cars a working day group-wide at c.6,000 units/year), which in Slack et al.'s "4Vs" terms places it at the low-volume, high-variety, high-visibility end — favouring craft/batch-like assembly, skilled labour and high unit cost, versus mass-market carmakers. (Inference from volume data above.)
- The two-plant structure is effectively product-focused (Gaydon = sports/GT and Specials; St Athan = SUV), a "focused factory" logic, with Newport Pagnell acting as an after-sales/heritage service operation.
- The 2020 collapse (3,394 units) followed by recovery to 6,000+ shows the operations system had to flex capacity sharply; the closure of a Gaydon line under Horizon indicates deliberate capacity reduction in favour of build-to-order.

## Q2. How is operations organised (leadership, employees) and what are its stated priorities?

### Takeaway
Manufacturing is led by a Chief Industrial Officer role created in 2023 (Vincenzo Regazzoni, ex-Ferrari Chief Manufacturing Officer), reporting into a CEO team that has turned over repeatedly: Andy Palmer (to 2020), Tobias Moers (Aug 2020–2022), Amedeo Felisa (2022–2024), Adrian Hallmark (from Oct 2024). Stated operational priorities have shifted from "Project Horizon" efficiency/build-to-order (2020) to supply-chain recovery and quality on new-model launches (2023–24) and, from 2025–26, cost reduction (headcount −20%, capex trimmed) alongside Valhalla ramp-up.

### Inferences
- Recruiting manufacturing leadership from Ferrari (Regazzoni; earlier Felisa as CEO) signals a deliberate move to import a "Ferrari-style" low-volume, high-margin, quality-led industrial model — an explicit attempt to make operations "externally supportive" (Hayes & Wheelwright stage 3–4) rather than merely neutral.
- The swing from hiring 400 technicians (Feb 2024) to cutting 20% of staff (Feb 2026) within two years illustrates poor capacity-demand alignment and high volatility in the operations plan — a dependability and cost problem in Slack's terms.
- CEO turnover (four CEOs in c.5 years) likely disrupted operations strategy continuity.

## Q3. How do operations support the competitive priorities of a luxury low-volume maker? (Performance objectives, order-winners/qualifiers, Hayes & Wheelwright)

### Takeaway
The evidence shows Aston Martin competing on exclusivity, design, performance and personalisation (order-winners), with quality and brand heritage as qualifiers; operations has delivered rising ASPs (£162k total ASP 2021 → £231k 2023) but has repeatedly failed on **dependability** (late parts, launch delays, Valhalla slippage) and **cost** (gross margin falling from 39.1% in 2023 to 29.4% in 2025). The "fewer cars, higher price" / build-to-order logic of Project Horizon is a demand-led strategy whose success depends on operations being dependable and flexible enough to deliver personalised cars on time.

### Inferences
- **Order-winners**: design, performance, exclusivity/scarcity (limited Specials such as Valkyrie/Valhalla), and personalisation (Q). **Qualifiers**: build quality, reliability, brand heritage, dealer/after-sales experience. - **Performance objectives ranking (inferred)**: Quality and Flexibility (mix/customisation) are strategically paramount; Dependability is a qualifier whose failure is now directly destroying value (2023–25 delays); Speed is secondary (buyers accept waiting lists, but not broken promises); Cost has become critical because low volumes give poor fixed-cost absorption (gross margin 39.1% → 29.4%).

## Q5. How do operations link to corporate strategy (Horizon, Stroll era, demand-led strategy, cost programmes, electrification/"Racing.Green") and what is the latest position (2024 AR, 2025 results, H1 2026)?

Corporate strategy since 2020 (Stroll/Yew Tree) has been to reposition Aston Martin as an ultra-luxury, demand-led brand with a renewed front-engined range (DB12, Vantage, Vanquish), the DBX SUV and high-margin Specials (Valkyrie, Valhalla), with electrification repeatedly deferred (first BEV moved from 2025 → 2026 → "before 2030" → reportedly 2033–35). Operations is the hinge: product launches and Specials deliveries drive ASP/margin, while delays have repeatedly derailed financial targets. H1 2026 shows recovery (wholesales +21%, revenue +38%, gross profit +68%) on Valhalla deliveries, with FY2026 guidance of volumes similar to 2025 (incl. c.500 Valhalla) and high-30s% gross margin, supported by a £550m refinancing.
