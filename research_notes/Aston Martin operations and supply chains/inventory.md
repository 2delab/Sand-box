## 1. What inventory does AML hold, how large is it by category, and how efficiently is it turned (2018–2025, H1 2026, Q3 2026)?

### Takeaway
AML reports three inventory categories: (i) parts for resale, service parts and production stock (≈ raw materials/bought-in components plus aftersales MRO stock; the largest category, c.53–58%), (ii) work in progress, and (iii) finished vehicles including company-owned service cars. On a consistent year-end cost-of-sales basis, AML's inventory days ran at ~91 days (2018), spiked to ~151 days in the 2020 pandemic/destock year, recovered to ~96 days (2021) and then drifted back up to ~110–114 days in 2024–2025 as wholesale volumes fell 10% in 2025; inventory per car wholesaled rose from ~£32k (2021) to ~£51k (2025) (calc.). Write-downs to NRV peaked at £24.2m in 2023.

### Inferences
- **Mapping to textbook categories:** "production stock" ≈ raw materials and bought-in components; WIP = cars on line or awaiting parts/rectification at Gaydon and St Athan; finished vehicles = finished goods (new cars not yet wholesaled plus service/demonstrator fleet); "parts for resale/service parts" = aftersales/MRO stock. Service cars were 60% of finished vehicles in 2023 (49.0/81.8), so true new-car finished-goods stock at the factory was only c.£33m — evidence that AML pushes finished cars to dealers quickly and that its "finished-goods" risk sits largely **in the dealer channel** (Section 2).
- **Trend:** DIO fell sharply from the 2020 crisis level (151 days) to 96 days in 2021 after the dealer destock and BTO reset, but has since deteriorated (≈110–119 days on average inventory in 2024–25). Inventory per car wholesaled rose ~60% between 2021 and 2025 (£31.9k → £51.0k), partly reflecting higher-value models/derivatives and Valhalla parts, but also slower throughput.
- **Seasonality:** inventory peaks mid-year (£337m Jun 2024, £354m Jun 2025, £313m Jun 2026) and falls at year-end as Q4 wholesales are pushed out; year-end ratios therefore flatter the average capital tied up. Using the June 2025 peak, DIO would be ~145 days on 2025 cost of sales (calc.).
- **Write-downs:** the £24.2m NRV write-down in 2023 (vs £8.9m in 2022 and £4.2m in 2024) coincides with the DB11→DB12 and Vantage/DBS→new Vantage/Vanquish changeovers, consistent with obsolescence of outgoing-model parts and service cars (inference; no extract states the cause).
- **CCC:** where computable (2022–23), AML's CCC was ~118–120 days, i.e. AML finances about four months of operations in working capital before deposits; customer deposits (£336m 2022, £272m 2023) offset much of this, which is why the deposit unwind in 2024 (−£178m) hurt cash so much.
- **Implied deposits:** subtracting the 2024 cash outflow (−£177.7m) and adding the 2025 inflow (+£2.6m) to the 2023 balance gives ~£94m (2024) and ~£97m (2025) (calc.; ignores FX/reclassification — verify against Note 21 of the 2024/2025 annual reports).

### Inferences
- **Inventory days are not AML's main weakness:** low-volume, high-personalisation luxury makers structurally carry 90–120 days (Ferrari, McLaren), far above volume OEMs (~33–37 days) or Porsche (~70–75 days). At Ferrari's 2025 DIO (117.7 days) AML would hold ~£286m on its 2025 cost of sales — almost exactly its actual £277.7m (calc.). At Porsche's 70 days it would hold ~£170m, releasing ~£107m (calc.).
- **The real gap is in cash conversion and margin:** Ferrari's ~90–124-day supplier credit and ~16–19-day collection give it a near-zero/negative CCC; AML pays faster (policy: within 90 days; actual ~53–59 days) and collects slower (incl. other receivables), giving a CCC ~4 months. Ferrari's finished goods (58% of its inventory) largely reflect allocated/pre-sold cars, whereas AML's channel risk sits with dealers and must be cleared with dealer support.
- **Direction matters:** Ferrari's DIO rose with growing revenue (+40% 2022–25), so its inventory build supports growth; AML's DIO rose while revenue fell 21% (2025), the "abnormal inventory" pattern that Chen et al. (2005) associate with poor shareholder returns.
- Caveats: AML's receivables are "trade and other" (overstates DSO); Ferrari's cost of sales includes financial-services interest; Porsche 2025 margin is depressed by one-offs; McLaren mixes entities.

## 5. Impact of inventory practices on operational and financial performance (FCF, liquidity, refinancing, pricing/dealer support, margin)

### Takeaway
Inventory and channel-stock decisions have repeatedly moved AML's cash flow, refinancing needs and margins: the 2019–20 dealer overstock forced destocks that coincided with FCF of −£338m and −£539m and an 18.2% gross margin (2020); the 2021 BTO reset and lean dealer stock delivered the best cash year (WC +£56m, FCF −£123m) and rising margins to 39.1% by 2023; in 2024–25 supply disruption, a £178m Specials-deposit unwind, a slower inventory turn and a US tariff-driven dealer overhang combined with falling volumes to cut gross margin to 29.4% and keep FCF at −£392m/−£410m, sustaining a cycle of high-cost refinancing (10–10.375% notes 2024; £550m HPS term loan July 2026). Destocking and Valhalla deliveries in 2026 coincided with gross margin recovering to 37% in Q3 2026 and FCF outflow nearly halving YTD.

### Inferences
- **Cash sensitivity:** each 10 days of DIO at 2025 cost of sales (£887.9m) ≈ **£24m** of cash (calc.); the 9M 2025 inventory build (£78m) alone equalled ~19% of that period's £415m FCF outflow, versus £25m (11% of £225m) in 9M 2026 (calc.).
- **Deposits outweigh inventory:** the 2024 Specials deposit unwind (−£178m) was >13× that year's inventory movement (+£13m); BTO Specials finance inventory up front, but the cash reverses on delivery, so Specials timing (Valkyrie/Valour/Valiant → Valhalla) is the dominant working-capital swing.
- **Refinancing link:** persistently negative FCF with c.£280–350m tied in inventory and ~4-month CCC forced 10%+ coupon debt (2024) and a super-senior term loan (2026); trimming DIO towards Porsche-like levels would have released c.£100m — meaningful against £145m of liquidity at June 2026 (calc./inference).
- **Margin link:** overstocked channels are cleared with dealer support (2025–26) and lower wholesale volumes reduce fixed-overhead absorption (overheads are absorbed on a normalised activity basis, so under-utilisation is expensed); the 2021–23 period of lean dealer stock coincided with margin expansion from 31% to 39%, while the 2025 overhang coincided with the fall to 29% — consistent with Eroglu & Hofer's finding that leanness pays, though causality cannot be isolated (tariffs, Specials mix and warranty also moved).
- **Evaluation:** strong practice in service parts (ToolsGroup), adequate DIO for the luxury segment, but weak channel discipline (three destocks in seven years), slower cash conversion than Ferrari, and exposure of lean sequenced inbound supply to supplier failures.
