## Q1. Structure, quantified: suppliers, purchasing spend, Mercedes share, sourcing geography, tier-1 names and single-source dependency

### Takeaway
AML is a low-volume assembler (about 5,400–6,600 wholesales a year, 2018–2025). Its two UK plants (Gaydon for sports and GT cars; St Athan in Wales for DBX) are fed mainly by continental-European tier-1s. Its core powertrain and E/E architecture come from one strategic partner, Mercedes-AMG. Bought-in materials are the dominant cost: "cost of inventories recognised as an expense" was about £652m out of £888m cost of sales in FY2025 [extract]. AML's own risk register names "reliance on single source vendors". No primary figure was found for supplier count, spend by country, or the £ value of purchases from Mercedes.

### Inferences
- **Cost of sales per car (derived):** £887.9m / 5,448 = **about £163k** (FY2025), against £1,000.0m / 6,030 = about £166k (FY2024). Over the same years core ASP rose from £177k to £185k. So **per-unit unit cost was roughly flat** while total volume fell 10%. The margin fall therefore came mainly from mix (fewer Specials), warranty and tariffs, not from rising unit cost.
- **Materials share (derived, subject to the column caveat):** £651.9m / £887.9m = about 73% of cost of sales (FY2025); £826.0m / £1,000.0m = about 83% (FY2024). Bought-in materials and components are the overwhelming cost driver, consistent with an assemble-and-specify business model.
- **Inventory cover (derived):** £277.7m / £887.9m × 365 = **about 114 days** of cost of sales (FY2025; FY2024 about 111 days). This is not lean in aggregate. Combined with very thin line-side stock (3–5 days in 2019), it implies high finished-goods and WIP inventory alongside lean parts call-offs.
- A 100-supplier SAQ coverage set suggests the **strategic tier-1 base is of the order of 100 firms**. This is an inference, not a disclosed supplier count.
- Mercedes is plausibly AML's single largest supplier by value (V8 engines for Vantage, DB12 and DBX, plus E/E), but this could not be quantified.

### Gaps
- **Not found:** total supplier count; purchasing spend by geography (UK/EU/RoW); £ purchases from Mercedes-Benz AG in the related-party note (the AR2025 note's amounts were not in any extract); labour cost within cost of sales. The 2015 Sustainability Report regional split (UK 26.5%, Europe 21%, Americas 29%) is **not clearly sourcing spend** (probably sales) and should not be used.
- **Not verified for core models:** Bosch, Continental, Magna, Brembo, Recaro, Eissmann, Multimatic (beyond Valkyrie) and Lucid hardware (none purchased yet; see Q5). The writer should not name them as suppliers.
- The V12 engine maker and location were not verified.

## Q2. Inbound and outbound logistics: providers, scope, sites and ports

### Takeaway
Inbound logistics is outsourced to **DHL Supply Chain**: appointed August 2017 for Gaydon, extended in February 2020 to the end of 2027 and expanded to the full St Athan inbound chain. DHL also provided Brexit port flexibility and a bio-LNG truck fleet. CEVA held the inbound contract before DHL (2009). Outbound logistics is far less documented. There is a UK enclosed-transporter case study (Russells), but no named ro-ro line or port contract could be verified.

### Cited Findings
- **DHL scope at Gaydon:** "in-bound transportation, inventory management, warehousing, and in-plant logistics", originally a five-year contract from August 2017 — [Logistics Manager, 2020](https://www.logisticsmanager.com/dhl-supply-chain-expands-contract-with-aston-martin/); [Automotive Logistics, 2020](https://www.automotivelogistics.media/dhl-expands-contract-with-aston-martin-at-st-athan/40303.article)
- **Extension and expansion (CILT UK, 24 February 2020):** the contract was extended to **end-2027** "following a successful two-year implementation". DHL was appointed to run the full inbound supply chain at **St Athan** for the DBX launch. AML's Jeremy Scoones cited DHL's "proven launch expertise and ability to provide an agile and cost effective solution across multiple sites" — [CILT UK, 2020](https://ciltuk.org.uk/News/Latest-News/ArtMID/6887/ArticleID/25348/DHL-expands-contract-with-Aston-Martin-to-2027-to-include-new-manufacturing-plant-in-St-Athan); [Motor Transport, 2020](https://motortransport.co.uk/dhl-supply-chain-extends-aston-martin-contract-to-2027-and-will-manage-new-south-wales-production-facility/5818.article); [Transport Advancement, 2020](https://www.transportadvancement.com/press-statements/dhl-expands-logistics-contract-with-aston-martin-to-2027/)
- **Warehouse and transport sites:** DHL introduced **8 bio-LNG trucks** to the AML operation, running from DHL's **Wellesbourne** (Warwickshire, near Gaydon) and **St Athan** sites, saving about **800 t CO₂ a year** — [Parcel and Postal Technology International](https://www.parcelandpostaltechnologyinternational.com/news/sustainability/dhl-supply-chain-introduces-8-bio-lng-trucks-into-aston-martin-fleet.html); [Fleet Point](https://www.fleetpoint.org/fuel/liquefied-natural-gas-lng/bio-lng-trucks-join-aston-martin-fleet-to-cut-carbon-emissions/)
- **Brexit role (January 2019):** AML was "signing deals with supplier DHL to allow for the use of ports other than Dover". The supply chain team was authorised to book air freight; the measures were approved at a December 2018 board meeting — [RTÉ, 7 Jan 2019](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2019/0107/1021755-aston-martin-brexit/); [Global Times, 2019](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/201901/1135198.shtml); [AJOT](https://www.ajot.com/news/aston-martin-board-approves-air-freight-in-brexit-planning)
- **Previous provider:** CEVA Logistics won the contract to "manage the full inbound supply chain including manufacturing support" (April 2009) — [Logistics Handling, 2009](https://www.logisticshandling.com/articles/2009/04/28/489-ceva-logistics-wins-contract-with-aston)
- **Outbound (UK):** Russells Transport case study: enclosed car transporters moved AML cars from Gaydon to UK storage compounds, export ports and air-cargo terminals during "a short-term increase in production volume" (undated) — [Russells Transport](https://www.russellstransport.co.uk/case-studies/enclosed-transport-for-aston-martin-lagonda/)
- **Outbound (North America):** Plycar describes itself as an "authorized Aston Martin carrier" for North America (self-description on its own site) — [Plycar Group](https://plycargroup.com/authorized-aston-martin-carrier/)
- **Port context:** Southampton (ABP) is "the UK's number one vehicle handling port", with on-site PDI and storage. No AML-specific contract was found — [ABP Automotive](https://www.abports.co.uk/services/automotive)

### Inferences
- The DHL "lead logistics provider" model (inbound transport, cross-dock/warehouse at Wellesbourne, line-feed) is a classic **lean-JIT enabler**. It lets AML run low line-side stock while outsourcing the buffer and transport function. Choosing DHL for the St Athan greenfield launch shows a **replicate-the-model** approach across both plants.
- The contract ends at **end-2027**, so a re-tender or renewal decision falls within the brief's horizon. This is relevant to any recommendations.
- Outbound flows to the US, which is the largest region (about 34% of 2025 wholesales), must cross the Atlantic, most likely by ro-ro from a south-coast port. This is an **inference only**: no carrier, port or transit time was verified.

### Gaps
- Whether the DHL contract was renewed or re-tendered beyond 2027 was not found. No 2024–2026 updates were found.
- **Outbound:** no verified ro-ro shipping line (for example Wallenius Wilhelmsen, Höegh, UECC, K Line), port (Southampton or Portbury), finished-vehicle logistics provider, or transit or order-to-delivery times.

## Q3. Downstream: dealer network, regional wholesale mix 2018–2026, Specials, ASPs, order book and dealer-stock events

### Takeaway
AML wholesales to about 156 almost entirely third-party dealers in 53 countries (2025), down from 168 in 54 countries (2019). The regional mix has swung sharply. APAC peaked at 28% of volume in 2021–22, then collapsed with China (China −47% in 2023, −72% in H1 2024) to 18% in 2025. The Americas became the anchor market (34% in 2025; +29% in H1 2026). Dealer stock has been actively managed several times: the 2019–20 destock of about 2,000 units, the 2025 US drawdown during the tariff pause, and 2026 dealer support to clear aged stock. Specials (182 units in 2025; about 500 Valhalla planned in 2026) carry the margin.

## Q4. Empirical record of disruptions and measured impact, 2019–2026

### Takeaway
AML has suffered a near-continuous run of supply-side and trade shocks. Measured impacts include:
- Brexit (2019): a £30m contingency and £9m of Brexit stock.
- COVID (2020): plants shut from late March, wholesales −42% to 3,394.
- Supply constraints (2021) and chips (2022): 350 unfinished DBX707s, more than £80m of delayed revenue in H1 2022.
- DB12 supplier readiness (2023): guidance cut from about 7,000 to about 6,700 units.
- Supplier fires, floods and insolvencies (2024): about 1,000 units lost.
- US tariffs, the China tax change and the JLR cyber spill-over (2025): gross margin fell to 29.4%, wholesales were 5,448, adjusted EBIT −£189m.

AML rarely discloses a £ figure for a single shock, so most impacts show up as volume and margin variances.

### Inferences
- The disruption sources have **migrated**: border friction (2019), then plant shutdown (2020), component scarcity (2021–22), supplier readiness at launch (2023), tier-2 physical and financial failure (2024), trade policy and shared-supplier cyber risk (2025). This fits Christopher and Peck's (2004) point that risk sits across supply-side, demand-side, process and control, and environmental dimensions. No single buffer protects against all of these.
- A low-volume OEM with single-source tier-1s is a **"price-taker" for allocation** in shortages. The 2024 episode (about 1,000 units, roughly 14% of pre-warning ambitions) compares with the Porsche case: Porsche lost a larger absolute volume but found alternatives within its VW group network. This is an inference, and AML's Novelis exposure is unconfirmed.
- The first-come-first-served quarterly US quota turns outbound logistics timing into a **cost lever**: shipping early in each quarter captures the 10% rate. This rewards S&OP that is synchronised with the quota calendar.

## Q6. Strategy classification (lean, agile, leagile) with academic grounding, and evaluation of responsiveness, cost-efficiency and competitiveness

### Takeaway
AML runs a **leagile hybrid that is mismatched to its product**.
- Upstream is lean: an outsourced DHL JIT inbound chain, low line-side stock, and shared Mercedes modules from single sources.
- The decoupling point sits at final assembly and specification for core cars, with dealer stock acting as a downstream buffer. For Specials, the chain is engineer- or build-to-order and pre-sold.
- In Fisher's (1997) terms the products are "innovative" (high margin, high variety, volatile demand), which calls for a responsive chain with strategic buffers.

The 2019–2025 evidence shows the mismatch. Repeated supply shocks became lost units (350, about 1,000, about 1,400 shortfalls), destocking and margin erosion (gross margin 36.9% to 29.4%). Competitiveness lags peers that run deeper order books: Ferrari's 29.5% EBIT margin against AML's −15%. The 2026 recovery (gross margin 35–37%) comes from the pre-sold, build-to-order Valhalla segment, the part of the chain that best matches Fisher's prescription.

### Inferences
**Classification**
- AML is **leagile**:
  - Lean, efficient and outsourced upstream: DHL, JIT, Mercedes modules, single sources.
  - Decoupling point at body-in-white and final assembly or specification for core cars.
  - Customer-order-driven configuration, plus a dealer-stock buffer for volume. The repeated destocks show that part of core volume is still built to forecast.
  - Engineer- and build-to-order for Specials, which matches Christopher and Towill's Pareto split.

**Fisher mismatch test**
- The innovative-product criteria are all met: high margin, short runs, extreme variety, and volatile regional demand (APAC swung from 1,815 to 968 in four years).
- The upstream chain is run on efficiency logic, so supply shocks turn directly into stranded WIP and lost volume rather than being absorbed by buffers. Examples: 350 DBX707s in 2022 (more than £80m), about 1,000 units in 2024, and the Q3 2025 shortfall.
- Meanwhile **aggregate** inventory is high (about 114 days of cost of sales). The buffers sit in the wrong place, in finished and aged dealer stock rather than in critical single-source parts. This is the core evaluative point for the essay (derived from Q1 and Q3 data).

**Responsiveness: weak to moderate**
- Commercial agility is real: the 2019–20 destock, the April–May 2025 US pause with dealer-stock drawdown, and duty cost-sharing.
- Physical and engineering responsiveness is poor: DB12 supplier readiness (2023), Valhalla about 4 years late and slow to ramp, the BEV deferred to 2033–35.
- Order-to-delivery for core cars appears to be about 6–8 months (anecdotal). That is long against the 3DayCar BTO benchmark (about 40 days in volume auto, with order processing the main delay), though acceptable for luxury.
- Ferrari's 18–24-month order book is a **deliberate demand-led scarcity buffer** that AML lacks for core models.

**Cost-efficiency: weak, but improving in 2026**
- Gross margin went 36.9% → 29.4% (2024 → 2025), then 35.2% (9M 2026).
- Cost of sales per unit was flat at about £163–166k while volumes fell 10%, so there is **no scale benefit**.
- Tariffs and warranty costs (about £65m) added cost, and inventory financing (about £39m a year) is a recurring cash cost.
- The Mercedes sharing deal clearly lowers development cost. The Lucid deal has so far been a sunk cost with no supply flowing.

**Competitiveness: weak in aggregate**
- AML's volume is about 40% of Ferrari's and its margin about 45 percentage points lower.
- Among Ferrari, Lamborghini, Bentley, Rolls-Royce and AML (45,630 units), AML holds about **12%** of volume. Ferrari holds about 30%.
- Peers with group backing (Lamborghini and Bentley under VW; Rolls-Royce under BMW) or a deep order book (Ferrari) absorbed 2024–25 shocks better. AML's network of independent partners gives access to technology but no shock-absorbing group supply network.

**Implications for recommendations (analytical)**
- Move buffers upstream to single-source, long-lead parts (Sheffi's redundancy).
- Develop second sources for at-risk tier-2 materials such as aluminium and electronics.
- Map visibility down to tier-2 and tier-3 (Christopher and Peck).
- Shift core cars toward order-backed scheduling to cut aged dealer stock (Holweg and Pil BTO).
- Run quota-aware S&OP for the US.
- Use the 2027 DHL renewal to add risk-sharing KPIs.
- Renegotiate the Lucid minimum spend.

### Gaps
- AML does not label its own strategy (lean, agile or otherwise); the classification is analytical.
- No AML-published order-to-delivery times, OTIF figures, supplier on-time delivery rates, or dealer stock in days.
- Peer figures for Bentley and McLaren come from lower-quality aggregators. Check against the VW Group 2025 annual report and McLaren Group accounts.
- No academic study specific to AML's supply chain was found.
